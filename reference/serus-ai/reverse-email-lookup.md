## Section: What it finds
- The breach dumps and credential collections the address appears in, with passwords and other sensitive values redacted until you choose to reveal them
- Accounts and profiles connected to the address across social profiles and specialist OSINT sources
- Other identifiers tied to the same details, such as usernames and domains
- For a business sender, whether the domain is a lookalike registered days ago rather than the real company's
- The source of every finding, so you can check the whole picture yourself

## Section: How it works
- 1 Enter the email address: Paste the address, or ask in plain language ("Who sent this invoice?"). Add a username or domain if you have one.
- 2 Serus searches many sources at once: Sources including the surface web, public records, social profiles, breach databases, paste sites and dark web datasets, in a single pass.
- 3 Review the connected results: Identifiers are cross-referenced into one profile, each finding linked to its source, with a low tolerance for false positives.
- 4 Decide what to do next: Confirm a payment change by phone before you wire, walk away from a seller, or rotate the passwords that leaked.

## Section: Frequently asked
- What is a reverse email lookup? A search that starts from an email address instead of a name. It shows the accounts, breach records and other identifiers tied to the address, so you can tell whether a sender is who they claim to be.
- Can I look up my own email address? Yes. Searching your own address shows which breaches it appears in and what's connected to it, so you can change exposed passwords and see what a stranger could find.
- Is a reverse email lookup legal? Working with publicly available information is legal in most places, but how you use it is governed by law. You need a legitimate reason, like fraud prevention or safety. Never use it for stalking or harassment, or for hiring, tenancy, credit or insurance decisions.
- Is what I search private? Yes. Nothing you search is used to train AI models, data is encrypted in transit and at rest, and we never sell your data. Serus doesn't contact the subject of a search.
