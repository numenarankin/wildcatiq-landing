## Section: In-depth guides (01 to 04)
- 01 What treasury management means for a growing company: Most companies split cash into two buckets. Operating cash covers payroll, rent, and vendors, and sits in checking earning nothing. Everything past that, sometimes called excess or idle cash, can go to work instead. Rho Treasury invests that second bucket in US Treasury Bills, a money market fund, and an ultra-short income fund, blended into one live rate that updates daily. Set a standing rule and cash moves back to checking on a schedule (weekly is the fastest cadence Rho supports for that direction). Recap bullets: keep operating cash accessible; keep reserves in FDIC-insured accounts; put idle cash into short-term instruments; formalize a treasury policy (instruments, limits, approvals, liquidity floor).
- 02 Money market accounts vs. a Rho Treasury account: Personal-finance "business money market" content is built for individuals; Rho Treasury is built for business entities (statements for your books, integrations, allocations from $50,000 into the tens of millions). It is not a bank account: held through SIPC-member broker-dealers (Apex for accounts opened after July 2024, Interactive Brokers before), SIPC-protected, not FDIC-insured. Trade-offs run both ways: a bank MMA is a deposit that doesn't fluctuate and is usually same-day; Rho Treasury yields vary and principal can lose value; investment income is taxed differently.
- 03 Rho Treasury vs. enterprise treasury management software: Enterprise TMS is for a dedicated treasury function across many bank accounts, entities, and currencies with forecasting and compliance workflows. Rho Treasury solves the narrower problem almost every company has: cash sitting idle in checking. No separate software, no treasury hire. Link: Read the treasury management software guide.
- 04 Choosing a treasury account for your stage: Rho Treasury starts to make sense once a company holds more cash than the next few months of spend require (often right after a round or once revenue outpaces burn). Below the $50,000 minimum, checking and a savings account cover it. Above it, the fee schedule and tiered yield both work in your favor as the balance grows. Link: Read the corporate treasury guide.
